ABSTRACT
Quality assessment of smart contract bytecode decompilation, vulnerability homology analysis, and
codeclonedetectionreliesonreliablemeasurementofbytecodesimilarity.Traditionalmethodsadopt
structural approximation metrics such as opcode edit distance and control-flow graph isomorphism
for fast measurement. However, the low-level structure of bytecode is strongly correlated with
compiler version and optimization level: bytecode generated from the same source under different
compilation configurations is often misjudged as dissimilar, while structurally similar bytecode may
be semantically opposite. Even deep learning approaches often generalize poorly due to the lack of
high-quality labeled data.
We propose the Static Interleaved Trace Metric (SITM). SITM builds a Static Interleaved Trace
(SIT) interleaving typed events: external calls, storage accesses, event emissions, exceptional reverts,
and returns. in bytecode layout order, adding fine-grained features (function selectors, storage base
slots, event signatures). Similarity uses type-weighted edit distance with globally fixed calibration
weights.Sincecallsdetermineassetflows,SITMappliesboundedsoftgatingtothecallsubsequence,
trading∼0.015overallAUCfordetectingsystematicmulti-callredirection.Single-pointcallhijacking
is not covered by default and requires symbolic/dynamic differential rechecking.
Four controlled experiments, a downstream task, and an ablation show that SITM reduces
optimizer-induced deviation by about 87% versus OED; on 2,000 semantic-opposite mutation pairs,
ROC-AUC is 0.97 (95% CI±0.02), outperforming structural baselines among training-free methods.
It complements an open-source supervised Transformer in cost, interpretability, and training-free
deployment, with better version retention and noise robustness; SIT raises reentrancy detection from
39% to 76%. SITM is a low-cost pre-screening metric for statically traceable contracts; it does not
claim formal equivalence, and hits need rechecking.

2. Related Work
2.1. Smart Contract Decompilation and

lation lies in binary program analysis. Foundational work
on binary decompilation (Cifuentes and Gough, 1995),
semantics-preserving structural analysis and control-flow
reconstruction (Brumley, Lee, Schwartz and Woo, 2013;

the measurement ruler itself as the research object.

2.2. Bytecode Equivalence Metrics
Code clone detection has systematic surveys in tradi-
tional languages (Roy and Cordy, 2007), and the capability
boundary of smart contract clone detection has also been
systematically evaluated (Wang, Wan, Chen, Zhang, Lo,
Xie and Yang, 2025). Recent techniques, applications, and
challengesofsourcecodesimilaritymeasurementandclone
detection have also been systematically reviewed (Zakeri-
Nasrabadi, Parsa, Ramezani, Roy and Ekhtiarzadeh, 2023).
Existing bytecode equivalence metrics are mainly divided
into three categories:
•String-level metrics: the most commonly used is nor-
malized opcode edit distance. It is simple to compute
butextremelysensitivetocompilationconfigurations,
and is widely used in decompilation effect evaluation
andcontractclonedetection.OpcodeN-gramsimilar-
ity is also widely used for large-scale contract clone
detection(Ragkhitwetsagul,KrinkeandClark,2018);
its essence is local frequency features.
•Structure-level metrics: including control-flow graph
isomorphism,basic-blockmatchingrate,andfunction
signature matching rate. They are more stable than
string metrics, but still significantly affected by com-
piler optimization.
•Function-level metrics: compilation pass rate and in-
terface matching rate. They can only verify syntactic
correctness and surface matching, and cannot verify
deep semantics.
The above metrics are essentially structural approxima-
tion metrics, and their core assumption that “similar struc-
ture implies semantic equivalence” has never been strictly
verified. A few works introduce manual evaluation as a
supplement,butthecostishighandthesamplesizeissmall,
so they cannot serve as standardized metrics.
Inthedeeplearningdirection,someworksattempttouse
GNNsorTransformerstoencodeCFGsoropcodesequences
intoembeddingvectorsandthencomputedistances.Forex-
ample,thestructuralcodeembeddingrouteencodesopcode
sequences into fixed-dimensional vectors and computes co-
sine similarity (Gao, Jiang, Xia, Lo and Grundy, 2020); the
interpretable graph embedding route encodes control flow
and code structure into graph vectors and gives attributable
similarity (Liu, Ma, Mu, Wei, Xu, Jiao and Ren, 2025).
In the bytecode-level supervised Transformer direc-
tion, the closest work is SmartTrans (Chen et al., 2025b).
SmartTrans introduces a Jump Target Prediction (JTP) pre-
training task on the BERT architecture, encodes control-
flow information into the Transformer attention mechanism
through parameter-shared jump embeddings, and then fine-
tunes function embeddings with contrastive learning. Its
paper reports function-level similarity retrieval Recall@1
of 83.54% and contract-level Recall@1 of 97.41%, and

2.3. Program Semantic Equivalence Verification
Strict methods for program semantic equivalence are
based on formal verification, including symbolic execution,
model checking, and logical reasoning. Symbolic execution
has been widely used in smart contract security analysis,
such as Oyente (Luu et al., 2016) and Mythril (ConsenSys,
2017), but is limited by state-space explosion and difficult
to apply in batches to large-scale contract datasets. Recent
empirical studies also systematically evaluate the impact of
Solidity versions on vulnerability detection tools (Iuliano,
Corradini,Pasqua,CeccatoandDiNucci,2025).Suchmeth-
ods are theoretically rigorous, but the computational cost is
extremely high, and they are only suitable for small code
fragments, not for batch evaluation of large-scale decompi-
lation datasets.
This paper does not pursue strict formal equivalence
proof. Instead, for large-scale bytecode analysis scenarios,
it proposes an approximate but stable, low-cost, compiler-
configuration-robust operational semantic metric, greatly
improving the robustness of semantic evaluation at an ac-
ceptable cost. Current analysis tools are highly heteroge-
neous. SmartBugs 2.0 provides a unified execution frame-
work for 19 tools, supporting three input modes: source
code, creation bytecode, and runtime bytecode (Di An-
gelo, Durieux, Ferreira and Salzer, 2023). Orthogonal to
formal reasoning routes, dynamic analysis and differential
fuzzing execute candidate bytecode under the same inputs
and compare runtime behavior to determine semantic con-
sistency. The conclusions are closer to real semantics, but
an executable environment and transaction generation are
required, and the cost grows linearly with the number of
contracts,makingitdifficulttocoverhundredsofthousands
of contracts. Code clone detection methods based on pro-
gram dependence graphs (PDGs) and deep learning have
also been systematically reviewed in traditional languages
(RoyandCordy,2007;Zakeri-Nasrabadietal.,2023).SITM
occupies the position of “static, task-specific-training-free,
batch-capable” in this spectrum, complementing dynamic,
symbolicexecution,andPDGmethodsincost,accuracy,and
interpretability, rather than replacing them.

3.2. Overview of the SITM Framework
The core design idea of SITM is that compiler opti-
mization and version differences mainly change internal
executiondetailsandstackoperations,withoutchangingthe
contract’s external operational semantics, and should not
disrupttherelativeorderofkeysemanticeventsinthestatic
bytecode layout. Based on this, SITM no longer separates
semantic layers into independent sequences, but constructs
a Static Interleaved Trace (SIT) interleaved according to the
static layout order of bytecode.
In this paper, a static interleaved trace refers to a single
sequence of typed events. Each event is labeled as one of
fivesemantictypes—call,store,log,exc,orret—andevents
are interleaved according to their static layout order in the

3.3. Static Interleaved Trace Construction
Preprocessing uniformly performs the following steps,
and all baselines also run under the same preprocessing:
1. Metadata stripping: identify and strip the standard
CBOR/Swarm/IPFS metadata area at the end of byte-
code.
2. Variable-length disassembly: linearly scan according
to the EVM instruction set specification, skip data
bytes following PUSH-family instructions, and avoid
misjudging data as opcodes.
3. Basic-blockpartitioning:usejumptargetsofJUMP/JUMPI
and fall-through boundaries of linear scanning as ba-
sic blocks; dynamic jumps, i.e., targets from runtime
computation,areregardedasunresolvablejumps,and
thecontainingbasicblockispartitionedbylinearscan
boundaries.
4. Stack backtracking: initialize an empty virtual stack
inside each basic block, and simulate stack opera-
tionsinstructionbyinstructionforward.Forconstants
resolvable within the block, including PUSH direct
pushes and same-block computed constants, extract
their static values; for dynamic values passed across
blocks or loaded from memory, mark them as[DYN]
symbols.
This design strictly limits the scope of symbolic execu-
tion to a single basic block, avoiding cross-block control-
flow explosion. In contrast, Vandal provides a register-style
intermediate representation (Brent, Jurisevic, Kong, Liu,
Gauthier, Gramoli, Holz and Scholz, 2018), precise static
modeling of EVM memory requires higher cost (Lagouvar-
dos, Grech, Tsatiris and Smaragdakis, 2020), and methods
that parse jumps and build precise CFGs based on symbolic
execution are also expensive (Contro, Crosara, Ceccato and
Dalla Preda, 2021); SITM chooses local stack backtracking
within basic blocks rather than a complete register-style IR,
which is an engineering compromise between precision and
batch computability.

Static order is not equivalent to dynamic execu-
tion order:in the presence of loop bodies and conditional
branches, static layout order and runtime order may be
inconsistent, and reentrancy detection is distorted. Precise

Approximate metric rather than strict proof:SITM
is a semantic approximate metric and does not constitute a
formal equivalence proof.
Blindspotforpureinternalcomputationmutations:when
mutations only involve internal mathematical operations
withoutchangingexternalcalls,baseslots,eventsignatures,
and exception patterns, they are still missed.
Boundary of dynamic slot representation:although
base-slot extraction and access-mode labels are introduced,
completely dynamic mapping-key differences are still ab-
stracted, and the SH dynamic-slot group detection rate is
only 11%.
Reducedequivalencerecognitionundermajor-version
compiler migrations: In the major-version migration sub-
set, 86.1% of bytecode pairs are still classified as equivalent
at the threshold of 0.6, while the remaining 13.9% are clas-
sified as non-equivalent, constituting false negatives under
the semantic-equivalence assumption of this experiment.
Although the average similarity score decreases to 0.78,
most pairs remain above the equivalence threshold. This
indicates that major-version compiler migrations introduce
greater challenges to similarity consistency, resulting in
a higher false-negative rate compared with minor-version
migrations.
Blind spot for single-point call hijacking:when a con-
tractcontainsmultiplecalleventsandonlyonetargetaddress
or selector is tampered with while other events remain
unchanged,𝑆 𝑐𝑎𝑙𝑙 remains above the soft-gating threshold
and the penalty is not triggered. SITM cannot detect it,
and symbolic execution or dynamic differential analysis is
required for secondary rechecking.
Training-based methods still have upper-bound ad-
vantages when labels are sufficient:SmartTrans has an
overall AUC of 0.98, better than SITM’s 0.97. When la-
bels are sufficient and maximum discrimination is pursued,
SmartTrans can be used; SITM is more suitable for large-
scale batch evaluation and unseen compilation configura-
tions.
5.3. Application Scenarios
The application of SITM is limited to the pre-screening
stage of contracts dominated by statically traceable events,
and high-risk hit samples are then rechecked by high-
precision models or humans: decompilation quality assess-
ment,asaunifiedsemanticbenchmarkforfaircomparisonof
different methods; contract homology detection, identifying
logicalhomologyacrosscompilationversions;vulnerability
fingerprint matching, performing secondary fine-grained
investigation after hitting based on operational semantic
features; on-chain contract clustering, classifying closed-
source contracts by business semantics.
6. Conclusion
This paper proposes the SITM framework to address
the lack of semantic metrics in the field of smart contract
decompilation. Unlike separating semantic layers into in-
dependent sequences, SITM constructs a static interleaved
trace interleaved according to the static layout order of
bytecode, guarantees symmetry through type-weighted edit
distance with globally fixed calibration weights, and natu-
rallyhandleslayerexistencethroughtraceconstruction;soft
gating constitutes a bounded nonlinear penalty based on the
criticality of cross-contract interactions.
Four controlled experiments, one downstream task vali-
dation, and one ablation analysis show that under optimizer
perturbation,SITM’saveragedeviationisrelativelyreduced
by about 87.5% compared with OED, ROC-AUC reaches
0.97, and the detection rate of reentrancy-type mutations
increases from 39% for the three-layer independent scheme
to 76%, demonstrating the substantial contribution of cross-
layer event interleaving to reentrancy detection. Compared
withSmartTransfine-tunedon19,200contracts,SITMdoes
notcomprehensivelysurpassit—SmartTransisslightlybet-
ter in overall AUC, i.e., 0.98 vs. 0.97, and in tail stability
underoptimizerperturbation,whileSITMiscomplementary
in large-version score retention, about 0.80 vs. 0.67, noise
robustness, and computational cost, and maintains white-
boxinterpretabilityandsymmetry.Disablingsoftgatingcan
increase AUC to about 0.99 but reduces systematic call hi-
jackingdetectionfrom91%to52%,showingthatthedefault
configuration is a recall-prioritized compromise that trades
a small amount of AUC and equivalence-pair stability for a
substantial improvement in detecting systematic redirection
of multiple call events.
Overall, SITM is suitable for large-scale batch pre-
screening of contracts dominated by statically traceable
events. It is not suitable for internal computation mutation
detection, contracts dominated by dynamic addresses and
dynamic slots, single-point call hijacking, or precise reen-
trancy judgment under deep loop branches. In particular,
it is not suitable as the final judgment or sole defense
line in security-critical scenarios. Future work will explore:
introducing CFG topological order to approximate dynamic
execution order; introducing data-flow-sensitive base-slot
propagation to reduce the storage hijacking blind spot; con-
structing a semantic mutation test set for pure computation
contracts; and building a hybrid architecture with SITM
as fast pre-screening and GNN embeddings for precise
judgment of edge samples.
